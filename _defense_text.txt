Chapter One 
INTRODUCTION 
 
1.1  Background of the Study 
Food safety remains a critical global public health concern. Globally, the World 
Health Organization (WHO) estimates that unsafe food causes 600 million illnesses and 
420,000 deaths annually, disproportionately affecting low -income regions and children 
(Pires et al., 2021; Keddy et al., 2025). In the Philippines, this risk is concentrated in wet 
markets (palengkes), which remain the primary source of affordable protein. While vital 
for food security, these open markets frequently lack proper temperature con trol. Studies 
confirm high rates of meat spoilage and bacterial contamination in local open markets 
driven directly by poor personal hygiene, contaminated processing tools, and prolonged 
environmental exposure (Kenaw Z., Ejeso A., Deresse D., & Olkeba BK., 2024). 
A specific contributor to this risk is the direct contact with raw meat during 
consumer inspection, which poses a significant health risk due to the potential for cross -
contamination. Pathogenic microorganisms can be transmitted through direct physical 
contact and contaminated environments, facilitating the spread of zoonotic and foodborne 
diseases (Tumelty et al., 2023). Compounding this is the absence of accessible, cost -
friendly, and objective tools for freshness evaluation at the point of purchase. The 
monitoring of meat freshness traditionally relies on sensory evaluation: the observation of 
color, elasticity, odor, and viscosity, which is subjective, non -quantitative, and unreliable 
(Fan et al., 2024). Consumers in wet market settings therefore have no  safe, standardized 
means of assessing freshness beyond informal touching, smelling, and visual inspection. 
The main alternatives used to assess meat freshness each carry disqualifying drawbacks. 
Laboratory-based microbiological methods, while precise, req uire 24 to 48 hours to yield

results and involve complicated, destructive processes (Fan et al., 2024), rendering them 
wholly inaccessible at the point of sale. Existing portable detection technologies similarly 
remain costly and technically demanding (Iqbal, M., Yousaf, J., Khan, A., & Muhammad,

human judgment is influenced by numerous variables that countering subjectivity is crucial

Despite these limitations, color remains the most scientifically grounded freshness 
indicator available to consumers. Meat color is determined by the relative amounts of 
deoxymyoglobin, oxymyoglobin, and metmyoglobin, pigments detectable through their 
reflectance characteristics. The dynamic equilibrium among these pigments under varying 
oxygen and temperature conditions makes accurate freshness assessment impossible 
through unaided visual inspection alone, underscoring the need for objective, instrument -
based color measurement (Ruedt, C., Gibis, M., & Weiss, J., 2023). Research confirms that 
the HSV color space is more robust than RGB for organic surface color analysis under 
variable lighting, as it isolates chromatic information from illumination artifacts (Sánchez, 
C., Orvañanos -Guerrero, M., Domínguez -Soberanes, J., & Alvarez -Cisneros, Y., 2023; 
Basak, J., Madhavi, B., Paudel, B., Kim, N., & Kim, H., 2022), and prior computer vision 
studies on meat freshness classification report benchmark accuracies of 8 5 to 99% under 
controlled imaging conditions (Arsalane, A., Klilou, A., & Barbri, N., 2024; Song, S., Guo, 
Q., Duan, X., Shi, X., & Liu, Z., 2024). A documented limitation of these systems is their 
reduced performance in real -world market environments due to inconsistent lighting 
conditions (Altim, M., Basalamah, A., & Kasman, K., 2025), a challenge that flash -based 
illumination standardization at the moment of capture is specifically designed to address. 
This study proposes a mobile-based, contact-free meat freshness assessment system 
integrating flash -controlled image capture with HSV color analysis and Z -score 
classification. By mitigating the need to physically handle raw meat, the system reduces 
cross-contamination risk at the point of purchase. By replacing subjective sensory

1.2  Statement of the Problem 
Meat safety at the point of retail purchase in Philippine wet markets remains 
inadequately monitored despite existing national regulatory frameworks. The Meat 
Inspection Certificate issued under Republic Act No. 10536 certifies meat only at the 
moment of slaughter and does not extend to the conditions under which it is subsequently 
displayed and sold. Once meat enters the retail phase, no continuous mechanism exists to 
monitor the progressive surface color and quality changes driven by oxidative deterioration 
and prolonged exposure to the uncontrolled ambient temperatures. No validated, 
consumer-accessible tool currently exists to provide an objective, contactless, and near 
real-time surface freshness signal for unpackaged pork, beef, and chicken at the poin t of 
purchase. Specifically, this study aims to answer the following questions: 
1. How can species-specific thresholds for assessing the surface freshness of beef, 
chicken, and pork be established and validated? 
2. How can a progressive web-based system be developed to provide contactless 
statistical anomaly detection and classification based on the surface freshness 
of beef, chicken, and pork for consumers at the point of purchase? 
3. How effective is the system in classifying the surface freshness of beef, chicken, 
and pork using established color thresholds? 
1.3  Objective of the Study 
This study aims to address the absence of an accessible and objective freshness 
verification tool for consumers purchasing unpackaged meat in Philippine wet markets. 
The study seeks to deliver a standardized contactless alternative to subjective sensory 
evaluation that is grounded in species -specific colorimetric reference data, requires no

1.3.1  General Objective 
The general objective of this study is to develop and evaluate EyeSariwa, a web -
based visual meat quality assessment system that classifies the surface freshness of beef, 
chicken, and pork as Fresh, Suspicious, or Stale through HSV colorimetry and Z -score 
statistical anomaly detection, providing Filipino wet market consumers with an objective 
and contactless freshness signal at the point of purchase. 
1.3.2  Specific Objectives 
To accomplish the general objective of this study, the proponents of this study aim 
to accomplish the following specific objectives: 
1. To establish species -specific HSV colorimetric thresholds for the surface 
freshness states (Fresh, Suspicious, and Stale) of beef, chicken, and pork 
through structured sampling validated against the assessment of a qualified 
meat quality professional. 
2. To develop a web-based system that captures standardized smartphone images 
of meat surfaces under forced LED flash illumination and applies Z -score 
anomaly detection to extracted HSV values to produce a three -state freshness 
classification (Fresh, Suspicio us, and Stale) output without requiring device 
installation. 
3. To evaluate the classification accuracy of the system's HSV -based Z -score 
anomaly detection against expert meat quality assessment across beef, chicken, 
and pork samples, and to assess its usability among wet market consumers in 
terms of ease of use and interpretability of output at the point of purchase.

1.5  Scope and Limitations 
1.5.1  Scope 
The study focuses on the development and validation of EyeSariwa, a web -based 
system designed for the visual freshness assessment of raw beef, chicken, and pork. The 
technical framework centers on a Progressive Web Application (PWA) that utilizes the 
smartphone’s rear-facing camera and integrated LED flash. This setup follows an Active

Illumination protocol to standardize lighting and mitigate environmental interference from 
ambient market settings. On the backend, a Python -based Flask server processes these 
images using HSV colorimetry and Directional Z-score statistical anomaly detection. The 
system’s primary function is to classify meat surface color into three distinct categories of 
Fresh, Suspicious, or Stale. 
The research follows a Developmental Research Design. Data collection involves 
surveys with meat consumers, food security students, and food security graduates to 
evaluate system usability and perceived effectiveness. Statistical treatments for these 
surveys include the Percentage Weighted Mean and the Z-Test Score. Furthermore, official 
validation is conducted through interviews and sample labeling with a meat quality 
professional. The expert provides the ground truth for the calibration images used to verify 
the system's accuracy for beef, chicken, and pork. 
For the establishment of the datasets for freshness thresholds, with resources and 
time constraints in mind, the proponents sampled 60 total raw meat parts. The 2 most sold 
and bought species -specific cuts were chosen by the proponents. More specifically, the 
meat used for sampling were 10 beef shanks, 10 beef sirloins, 10 chicken drumsticks, 10 
chicken breasts, 10 pork belly, and 10 pork chops. Each sample was captured with an active 
flash in 4 different lighting conditions—outdoors, cool tone LED, warm tone LED, and red 
tone LED. Additionally, 2 samples from each meat cut were subjected to 3 different 
environmental conditions in varying time ranges. Samples were dipped in blood, left 
exposed to air, and left exposed to sunlight. To ensure the quality and f reshness of the 
samples, the meats were slaughtered on the same day the meats were photographed for data 
collection.  
1.5.2  Limitations 
The assessment evaluates surface -level visual discoloration of meats exclusively, 
excluding innards or visceral organs, and does not detect microbiological contamination,

1.6  Definition of Terms 
The following terms are defined conceptually and technically as they relate to the 
context of this study: 
Active Illumination . A computer vision protocol involving the mandatory 
activation of a smartphone’s built -in LED flash during image capture to impose a 
standardized, daylight -approximating illuminant on the meat surface. This technique is 
designed to override deceptive ambi ent market lighting and ensure consistent color 
extraction. 
Directional Z -Score. A statistical anomaly detection method where a sample's 
deviation from a verified fresh baseline is measured along specific axes of the HSV color 
space. This approach distinguishes between natural oxidative spoilage, characterized by 
shifts toward brown or yellow hues, and suspected adulteration, indicated by hyper -
saturated red levels. 
EyeSariwa. The specific title of the proposed web -based visual meat quality 
assessment system is designed to classify beef, chicken, and pork into three distinct

Pamantasan ng Lungsod ng Maynila 
freshness categories (Fresh, Suspicious, or Stale) using smartphone-based colorimetry and 
statistical analysis. 
Freshness Threshold. A calibrated numerical limit derived from expert-validated 
baseline data of fresh meat samples. This value serves as the statistical benchmark for the 
Z-score logic, determining a sample’s classification based on its level of deviation from 
established chromatic norms. 
HSV Color Space . A cylindrical -coordinate representation of points in an RGB 
color model, consisting of Hue (chromatic identity), Saturation (color purity), and Value 
(brightness). This model is utilized to isolate the intrinsic "redness" signal of meat tissue 
from external illumination artifacts and luminance variations. 
Image Segmentation. The algorithmic process of partitioning a digital image to 
isolate the meat surface, or Region of Interest (ROI), from background elements such as 
chopping boards, retail surfaces, or packaging. This ensures that feature extraction and 
colorimetric analysis are performed exclusively on the meat tissue. 
Meat Inspection Certificate (MIC) . A regulatory document issued by the 
National Meat Inspection Service (NMIS) certifying that meat was safe and wholesome at 
the time of slaughter. This study identifies the limitations of this certificate as a point -in-
time assessment that does not account for post-slaughter degradation. 
Myoglobin. The iron-rich heme protein responsible for the pigmentation of muscle 
tissue. The system tracks the biochemical transition of myoglobin through three natural 
states—Oxymyoglobin (bright red), Deoxymyoglobin (purplish -red), and Metmyoglobin 
(brown)—to serve as primary visual indicators for freshness assessment. 
Point-of-Purchase (PoP) . The specific time and location of where a retail 
transaction is completed. This study focuses on the PoP as the critical moment for 
intervention, allowing consumers to make informed safety decisions before the final 
transfer of goods.

Progressive Web Application (PWA) . A software delivery model built with

Republic Act No. 9296 (Meat Inspection Code). The primary national legislation

REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE 
 
2.1  Related Literature 
2.1.1  Foreign Literature 
Meat Freshness, Microbial Growth, and Food Safety 
Meat, including beef, chicken, and pork, is a highly perishable commodity whose 
quality and safety decline rapidly due to microbial activity and biochemical changes. Its 
high moisture content, abundant nutrients, and near -neutral pH provide an optimal 
environment for microbial growth and lipid oxidation, both of which significantly reduce 
shelf life and compromise meat quality (Uhlig , E., Bucher, M., Strenger, M., Kloß, S., & 
Schmid, M., 2024). Microbial activity is a key driver of meat spoilage, wherein sp oilage 
and pathogenic microorganisms such as Pseudomonas, Listeria monocytogenes , and 
Salmonella spp. multiply during storage and handling, resulting in quality deterioration 
characterized by off -odors, discoloration, and surface slime (Liu et al., 2023; Zhu et al., 
2022). Evidence shows that these microorganisms can grow under both refrigerated and 
temperature-abused conditions, with similar final microbial loads observed across different 
meat types despite variations in initial contamination levels, indic ating strong microbial 
adaptability across food matrices (Liu et al., 2023). Meat spoilage is therefore understood 
as a microbial ecological process driven by interacting microbial communities and 
environmental conditions, with bacterial activity playing a  more dominant role than 
endogenous enzymatic degradation (Zhu et al., 2022). 
Advances in molecular techniques, such as 16S rRNA sequencing, further 
demonstrate that microbial community composition changes in predictable succession 
patterns as meat transitions from fresh to spoiled. This reinforces the concept that spoilage

is not random but follows structured microbial dynamics, meaning meat quality can be 
proactively detected based on observations (Dorn -In, S., Mang , S., Cosentino , R., & 
Schwaiger, K., 2024; González-Fandos, E., Laorden, A., Usón, S., & Andrés, M., 2025). 
This is why ensuring meat freshness is essential, as it directly affects consumer health and

H., Chen , Q., & Kong , B., 2023). Contaminated meat, possibly harbori ng harmful 
biological and chemical agents which contributes to more than 200 diseases worldwide, 
ranging from diarrhea to cancer (WHO, 2024).  
A critical yet frequently underappreciated dimension of microbial risk in meat 
handling is the role of food handlers as vectors of cross-contamination. Handling raw meat 
with bare hands facilitates the transfer of pathogens to other surfaces, equipment, 
packaging materials, and even the handler’s own face —particularly the eyes, nose, and 
mouth. Studies have identified bacterial contamination on both the hands and nasal 
passages of food handlers during routine tasks, highlighting the persistent risk of pathogen 
transmission (Sharma , A., Gangopadhyay , S., Agarwal , J., & Kumar , A., 2021). 
Furthermore, individuals carrying toxin -producing bacteria may act as reservoirs for 
contamination, posing a direct risk of staphylococcal foodborne illness (Bencardino , D., 
Amagliani, G., & Brandi, G., 2021). In retail meat environments, improper practices such 
as bare-hand contact with raw meat remain a recurring hygiene issue with documented 
cross-contamination consequences (Oliveria, I., Almeida, M., Gomes, J., & Henriques, A., 
2024). 
These combined risks highlight the broader challenge of maintaining food safety 
while minimizing direct contact with raw meat. Consequently, there is a growing need for 
reliable, objective, and contactless methods for assessing meat freshness and quality. A 
Hue-Saturation-Value (HSV) colorimetry-based approach addresses this need by enabling 
rapid, non-destructive freshness evaluation through image capture. This reduces the need 
for physical handling, minimizes the risk of pathogen transmission, and contrib utes to

Physicochemical and Color Changes in Meat During Deterioration 
 When meat deteriorates, it undergoes a series of physicochemical changes that 
affect its composition, texture, water-holding capacity, and color. Central to these changes 
is myoglobin, which exists in three redox states: deoxymyoglobin (purplish -red), 
oxymyoglobin (bright cherry -red associated with freshness), and metmyoglobin (brown 
discoloration associated with spoilage) (Soladoye et al., 2015; Mancini & Hunt, 2005, as 
cited in Pujol , A., Ospina-E, J., Alvarez, H., & Muñoz , D., 2023). Lipid oxidation and  
myoglobin oxidation are closely interconnected processes, as secondary lipid oxidation 
products accelerate the formation of metmyoglobin, resulting in a mutually reinforcing 
process that drives visible discoloration in stored meat (Wang et al., 2021). Imp ortantly, 
the extent and pattern of these oxidative changes vary across meat species due to 
differences in myoglobin content and lipid composition (Han et al., 2024; Geng L., Liu K., 
& Zhang H., 2023). 
These biochemical differences result in distinct color degradation pathways among 
meat types. In beef, oxymyoglobin gives the characteristic bright red appearance, which 
gradually shifts to brown as metmyoglobin accumulates during oxidation (Krell et al., 
2024). Pork, with lower myoglobin content, typically transitions from pink to grey-brown 
as both pigment and lipid oxidation progress (Gagaoua , M., Suman, S., Purslow , P., & 
Lebret, B., 2023). In contrast, chicken contains minimal myoglobin, making lipid oxidation 
the dominant factor in visual deterioration, often resulting in yellowing and surface 
dullness rather than a red-to-brown transition (Hearn et al., 2021). 
In addition to species differences, color variation is also influenced by anatomical 
cut, as different muscle types vary in fiber composition, metabolic activity, and myoglobin 
concentration (Barkley , K., Boler , D., & Harsh , B., 2023; Zhang et al., 2026). These

species-specific color profiles highlight the need for meat-type-sensitive approaches, as the 
visual indicators of deterioration are biochemically and chromatically distinct across beef, 
pork, and chicken —a distinction that Eye-Sariwa must account for in order to deliver 
accurate and reliable freshness evaluations across all three meat types.  
Limitations of Sensory-Based Meat Freshness Evaluation 
 Human sensory evaluation has long served as the primary method by which 
consumers assess the freshness of meat at the point of purchase, yet a growing body of 
literature reveals that this approach is structurally unreliable. Ruiz -Capillas, C., Herrero, 
A., Pintado, T., and Delgado -Pando, G. (2021) documented that sensory analysis is 
inherently subjective, shaped by cultural background, education, habits, and individual 
differences in sensory capacity, all of which produce inconsistent judgments across 
evaluators. This variability is not limited to untrained consumers, as significant 
inconsistency has been observed even among trained panels when selection criteria, 
training protocols, and testing conditions are not tightly controlled (Anagnostou, G., 
Ferragina, A., Crofton, E., Celayeta, J., & Hamill, R., 2025). Beyond individual 
differences, purchase decisions are frequently influenced by non -sensory extrinsic factors 
such as price, product origin, and packaging logos that can override actual freshness cues 
entirely (Cardona, M., Izquierdo, D., Barat, J., & Fernández-Segovia, I., 2023). Freshness 
discrimination from appearance has also been shown to improve with buying experience, 
meaning inexperienced shoppers are systematically less accurate in their assessments (Liu 
et al., 2024). While in low - and middle-income informal market settings, consumers and 
vendors tend to rely on environmental heuristics such as buyer volume, the presence of 
flies, and surface warmth as proxies for freshness rather than on any m icrobiological 
understanding of spoilage (Kuboka et al., 2024). 
The safety consequences of sensory evaluation's unreliability are well-documented 
and directly relevant to the Philippine wet market context. Viana et al. (2022) found that 
fish consumers continued to rate products as acceptable for purchase at approximately 4.1

days of storage, a point at which samples were already microbiologically unsuitable for 
consumption, demonstrating that visual and olfactory cues frequently lag behind the actual 
spoilage threshold. Divergent neurological responses to the same odor stimuli  between 
individuals, mediated by emotional states, cultural familiarity, and prior experience, further 
reduce the consistency of sensory-based spoilage detection (Ciobanu et al., 2023). On the

evaluation panels are feasible at the point of sale, a smartphone -based system that applies 
statistical analysis to captured surface color data represents a practical bridge between the 
inadequacy of pure sensory evaluation and the impracticality of laboratory-grade methods. 
Environmental and Human-Made Factors Affecting Meat Appearance   
The physical environment in which meat is displayed exerts a strong and 
measurable influence on its surface color, which is the primary cue consumers use to assess 
freshness. Meat color is governed by myoglobin chemistry, specifically the conversion of 
red oxymyoglobin to brown metmyoglobin through oxidation driven by exposure to 
oxygen, light, and elevated temperature (Tushar, Z., Rahman, M., & Hashem, M., 2023). 
Higher ambient temperatures intensify this oxidative process, decreasing redness and 
increasing lightness and yellowness in a pattern corresponding to visible discoloration, 
with chicken being especially susceptible due to its lower myoglobin content and higher 
proportion of unsaturated fat (Lesiów, T., & Xiong, Y., 2024). A meta-analysis on poultry 
confirmed that rising ambient temperatures consistently produce lighter, less red, and more

yellow meat, following the same oxidative pathway across production conditions 
(Leishman et al., 2021). Open -air aerobic conditions compound these effects, as 
unrestricted oxygen exposure has been shown to accelerate pigment re-oxidation and drive 
rapid, m easurable loss of redness even at refrigerated temperatures (Zdunczyk, W., 
Modzelewska-Kapituła, M., & Tkacz, K., 2024), and aerobically displayed ground pork 
has been found to exhibit significant color deterioration across storage time compared with 
oxygen-restricted samples (Ham, Y., Song, D., & Kim, H., 2022). Elevated temperature 
combined with open-air exposure also strongly accelerates the growth of spoilage bacteria

reduces the influence of one channel's variation on the others and produces more stable 
feature-quality relationships across varying lighting conditions. 
CIELab, the standard color space in food science colorimetry, offers perceptual 
uniformity by separating lightness from chromatic components in a way designed to align 
with human visual perception, and studies comparing multiple color spaces for meat 
freshness classification generally find it yields slightly higher or more consistent accuracy 
than HSV under controlled conditions (Sánchez et al., 2023). However, the practical 
deployment of CIELab in consumer -grade imaging contexts is constrained by its

2023). A beef freshness classification study using white-box machine learning across RGB, 
HSV, an d CIELab feature sets found that while CIELab performed best when used in 
isolation, combining features across all color spaces produced the highest overall accuracy. 
Suggesting that no single color space is universally superior and that the choice must be

METHODOLOGY 
 
3.1 Research Design 
The classification of this study as developmental research follows from the nature 
of its primary output and the scope of its research questions. Developmental research in 
software and system development contexts centers on the design, construction, and 
evaluation of a functional artifact intended to address a specific problem within a defined 
local setting, with quality judged through assessments of usability and effectiveness rather 
than through the production of generalizable theoretical knowledge (Rachma and Muhlas, 
2022). EyeSariwa satisfies this definition across all three specific objectives, moving from 
reference data construction and system development through empirical evaluation within 
the target deployment context. The study was not classified under Design Science Research 
because that framework requires the articulation of design principles that remain valid and 
applicable beyond the system built, which are lessons other developers could apply when 
building entirely different systems in different  contexts (Betz et al., 2024). The present 
study's scope is bounded by the development and evaluation of EyeSariwa within 
Philippine wet market conditions and does not extend to producing such transferable design 
knowledge. 
This study employed a developmental research design, structured across two 
sequential phases: a system development phase and a system evaluation phase. 
Developmental research is appropriate for studies whose primary output is a functional 
technological art ifact, as it centers the research process on the iterative construction, 
refinement, and empirical assessment of a system designed to address a documented 
problem. In the case of EyeSariwa, the documented problem is the absence of an accessible, 

===== PAGE 51 =====

Pamantasan ng Lungsod ng Maynila 
objective contactless freshness verification tool for consumers purchasing unpackaged 
meat in Philippine wet markets. 
The development phase encompassed two interconnected activities: the 
establishment of species -specific HSV colorimetric reference ranges through structured 
sample collection and expert validation, and the design and implementation of the 
EyeSariwa web -based system using the established reference ranges as the statistical 
foundation for classification. The evaluation phase involved assessing the system along 
two dimensions through classification accuracy, measured by comparing system output 
against expert meat quality assessment on a separate test sample set, and system usability 
measured through the System Usability Scale administered to wet market consumer 
respondents following supervised system use. This two-phase structure directly mirrors the 
three specific objectives of the study and provides a coherent methodological framework 
that moves from data grounding through system construction to empirical validation.

surface browning, or yellowing are excluded, as inclusion of ambiguous samples would

Sample collection targeted 60 meat samples in total, sourced from a registered meat

the distribution chain at a point closer to slaughter, ensuring that samples had undergone 
minimal post-slaughter handling and storage time prior to capture. The sample collection 
strategy differs across species. For chicken, the researchers collec ted 10 Fresh samples 
each from two anatomical cuts — breast and drumstick — yielding 20 Fresh chicken 
samples in total. For pork, 10 Fresh samples each were collected from two cuts — pork

were collected from two cuts — sirloin (tapa slice) and beef shank (bulalo) — yielding 20 
Fresh beef samples. This cut -level stratification is necessary because distinct muscle 
groups carry different proportions of muscle fiber types and myoglobin content, resulting 
in measurable differences in the surface color profile of the Fresh state across cuts (Barkley 
et al., 2023). Separate HSV reference distributions are therefore computed for each species-
cut combination rather than pooling all cuts within a species into a single distribution. As 
seen in the feedback loop of Figure 3, the sample collection cycle repeats for each cut until 
the target count is met before the process advances to the computation phase. 
All samples are sourced and photographed on the same session day to prevent post-
purchase deterioration from influencing the captured color values before photography. For 
bone-in cuts such as chicken drumstick and beef shank, the full cut is captured as presented 
in the frame. The center -crop extraction performed during HSV processing ensures that 
only exposed muscle surface color is analyzed, excluding peripheral areas that may contain 
bone, fat cap, connective tissue, or background elements.

aggregating HSV statistics across these varied lighting conditions rather than restricting 
data collection to a single controlled illumin ation source, the resulting reference 
distributions inherently account for the colorimetric shifts introduced by different light 
spectra, reducing the likelihood of false rejections when the live system is deployed under 
lighting conditions that differ fro m those used during reference data construction. Meat 
samples are placed on a white or neutral-grey surface during capture to prevent background
